The Jewel of East Africa

The World's Premier Safari Destination

Kenya is not just a destination — it is an awakening. It is the golden dust dancing in the morning light, the thunderous hooves of the Great Migration, and the quiet dignity of a lion at rest in tall savannah grass.

From the snow-capped peak of Mount Kenya to the endless plains of the Masai Mara, from the flamingo-stained shores of Lake Nakuru to the ancient riverbeds of Samburu — every horizon promises untamed beauty and raw, visceral life. Kenya is where the wild still roams free.

Kenya's Icons

The Big Five

Hover any card to reveal the story behind the animal.

Lion
Masai Mara National Reserve

A lion's roar can be heard up to 8 kilometres away — both a territorial claim and a family call.

Leopard
Samburu National Reserve

Leopards are strong enough to drag prey twice their own weight up into a tree to protect it from scavengers.

African Elephant
Amboseli National Park

Elephants mourn their dead and have been observed returning to the bones of lost family members for years.

Cape Buffalo
Masai Mara National Reserve

Cape buffaloes have exceptional memories — they've been known to track and ambush hunters who previously wounded them.

Black Rhinoceros
Ol Pejeta Conservancy

Kenya is home to the world's largest population of critically endangered black rhinos — with fewer than 6,000 remaining.

Jan – Mar

Dry Season

  • Calving season in Masai Mara (Jan–Feb)
  • Excellent elephant viewing in Amboseli
  • Samburu — predators active at rivers
  • Clear Kilimanjaro views

Jul – Oct

Peak Safari Season

  • Great Wildebeest Migration river crossings
  • Flamingo spectacle at Lake Nakuru
  • Best all-round game density
  • Dry roads — most parks accessible

Nov – Dec

Short Rains & Festive

  • Migratory birds arrive (Oct–Nov)
  • Lush green scenery
  • Fewer crowds, lower rates
  • Festive season peak in December
